## Authority statement

This Standard governs the Behavioral Evidence Report and the Recorded Conduct Entries it carries: why the report is structured as it is, how entries are authored and amended for Dimensions D1 to D14, and how the report face is built.

Every Dimension Execution Edition carries a Recorded Conduct Library that must comply with this Standard. Where an entry in a published Execution Edition does not comply, it is recorded in the Standing Defects Register at Section 47 and held for recalibration. It is not corrected in place while the Execution Edition is frozen for build.

Where this Standard intersects an approved governing instrument, that instrument retains its established authority. This Standard does not authorize a new observation rule, evidence state, reporting feature, research use, or commercial claim.

# PART A — WHY RECORDED CONDUCT IS BOUNDED

Part A sets out the institutional rationale. It is not separately circulable. This Standard is classified Strictly Confidential and Founder Owned in whole. Where the rationale is required outside that classification, a controlled derived edition must be authorized and issued under its own document identifier.

## 1. The proposition

**¶1.1**  The Behavioral Evidence Report documents selected conduct observed or stated within defined workplace situations under governed conditions. Its purpose is not to construct a comprehensive behavioral portrait, assign personal attributes, predict workplace performance, or determine employment suitability.

**¶1.2**  The 3rd Academy has adopted bounded, standardized recorded conduct rather than unrestricted observer narratives or broad assessments of the person.

**¶1.3**  That decision serves five institutional objectives: constraining observer interpretation, making calibration possible within explicitly defined decision rules, separating conduct from situational consequences, supporting the portability of evidence across workplace contexts, and limiting unsupported inferences about participants.

**¶1.4**  Three properties must be distinguished and must never be treated as one.

**¶1.5**  **Rendering reproducibility** concerns whether identical authorized determinations produce identical report entries.

**¶1.6**  **Observer agreement** concerns whether independent observers applying the same rules to the same available evidence reach the same determinations.

**¶1.7**  **Factual adequacy** concerns whether those determinations and entries accurately represent what the underlying evidence establishes.

**¶1.8**  Achieving one property does not establish the others.

**¶1.9**  The central thesis is:

> The credibility of a Behavioral Evidence Report depends not on how much can be said about a participant, but on how consistently, accurately, and defensibly the institution can establish what the available evidence permits it to say.

**¶1.10**  Bounded reporting is an intentional institutional design choice. Its scientific and operational performance must be established through controlled testing, not presumed from the simplicity of its wording.

## 2. The foundational problem: observations do not interpret themselves

**¶2.1**  Every behavioral observation involves five distinguishable elements.

**Table 2.1. The five distinguishable elements of a behavioral observation.**

| Serial | Element |
|---|---|
| 1 | The situation in which the participant was given an opportunity to respond |
| 2 | The information, instructions, constraints and conditions available to the participant |
| 3 | What the participant actually stated or did |
| 4 | The observer's determination about that response |
| 5 | The entry subsequently admitted into the institutional evidence record |

**¶2.2**  These elements are related. They are not interchangeable.

**¶2.3**  Two observers examining the same situation may describe the same response differently. One may emphasize courage, another communication, a third ethical awareness. Their descriptions may concern the same event while introducing different interpretations of its significance.

**¶2.4**  That becomes an institutional problem when those descriptions enter a portable document an employer may use when considering consequential matters. An unrestricted reporting process permits variation arising not from the participant's conduct but from the observer's vocabulary, assumptions, experience, interpretive preferences or expectations.

**¶2.5**  The 3rd Academy addresses this by constraining the interpretive step between the governed observation and the final report.

**¶2.6**  Observers are not authorized to construct general behavioral portraits or decide what kind of person the participant is. They are responsible for accountable determinations about whether specified conduct propositions are supported by the available evidence under the applicable rules.

**¶2.7**  A record that a participant identified an issue and communicated what it was describes a documented act. A conclusion that the participant possesses integrity, courage or dependable ethical judgment makes a broader claim. The second does not follow automatically from the first.

**¶2.8**  This is consistent with the methodological importance of examining the inferences and assumptions connecting observed responses with their intended interpretations (Kane, 2013).

> The 3rd Academy's reporting authority extends only as far as its governed evidence and approved interpretation rules permit.

## 3. Why bounded entries make the calibration task explicit

**¶3.1**  When independent authorized observers examine the same complete available source record under the same governing definitions and library version, they should reach the same supported determination where the evidence is sufficiently clear. Their conclusions should not differ because one favors elaborate narrative and another restrained description.

**¶3.2**  Consider a controlled situation in which a participant identifies a material issue and communicates its existence. Three observers might write the following.

**Table 3.1. Three descriptions of the same response.**

| Serial | Observer | Possible description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | The participant demonstrated exceptional ethical courage |
| 2 | B | The participant showed professional integrity by challenging wrongdoing |
| 3 | C | the participant stated that a specific issue existed and identified what it was |

**¶3.3**  The first two introduce interpretations of personal qualities or wider significance. The third stays close to the particular act the evidence may establish.

**¶3.4**  That does not make the third automatically correct. It must still satisfy the approved admission conditions. Its institutional advantage is that those conditions can be specified, reviewed, applied repeatedly and tested against reference cases.

**¶3.5**  The third description is ST-D1-011, the first entry in the D1 Recorded Conduct Library. It is used as the worked example throughout this Standard.

**¶3.6**  A controlled library reduces the permissible range of report language and makes the applicable decision rules explicit. It does not eliminate observer judgment, and it is not the only possible route to calibration. Narrative observation can also be governed through structured frameworks, reference examples, training, review and accuracy checks. Research in medical education has shown narrative observation quality improving after rater training while some inaccurate observations increased (Kogan et al., 2023). Those findings do not establish what will occur here. They show why standardized and narrative reporting must not be treated as a simple opposition between reliable and unreliable methods.

**¶3.7**  Accordingly:

> The same governed evidence should produce the same defensible recorded conduct determination under the same rules, while disagreement and factual error must be examined as separate phenomena.

**¶3.8**  Calibration must not manufacture consensus. Where evidence is ambiguous, unavailable or inadequate, the authorized response may be to withhold the entry and record the applicable limitation.

## 4. The distinction between conduct and consequences

**¶4.1**  Similar conduct may produce different situational consequences. A person who communicates an uncomfortable issue may meet a constructive response, defensiveness, or no response at all. The reactions of other people do not establish differences in the focal conduct.

**¶4.2**  If the report emphasized favorable or unfavorable consequences instead of the participant's documented response, conditions outside the participant's control would influence what is admitted.

**Table 4.1. Comparable conduct under differing subsequent reactions.**

| Serial | Participant | Focal documented conduct | Subsequent reaction | Principle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | Identifies an issue and communicates it | Manager responds constructively | Record the supported conduct |
| 2 | B | Identifies a comparable issue and communicates it | Manager responds defensively | Record the supported conduct |
| 3 | C | Identifies a comparable issue and communicates it | Manager takes no immediate action | Record the supported conduct |

**¶4.3**  This requires a qualification. Not every action occurring after the focal response is irrelevant. An interruption, a refusal, a revised instruction or the disclosure of new information may change the conditions available to the participant before a subsequent act. Such events are not merely outcomes; they may become material context for interpreting what the participant does next.

**¶4.4**  The rules therefore distinguish four things: the participant's focal act, another person's subsequent reaction, a change in conditions affecting the participant's next opportunity, and the participant's later act under those changed conditions.

**¶4.5**  An initial disclosure made without prompting and a later disclosure made after direct questioning may support a common proposition that an issue was communicated. They do not support the stronger proposition that disclosure was consistently self initiated.

**¶4.6**  The institution must not introduce undefined judgments about communication manner. Permissible distinctions concern authorized, observable features: addressee, channel, stated content, sequence, or source defined timing. Accent, fluency, perceived confidence, polish or cultural style must not become conduct findings because an observer noticed them.

> The report documents what the participant stated or did under defined conditions, not whether the surrounding situation rewarded the response.

## 5. Portability, and its five distinct meanings

**¶5.1**  The report is intended to accompany a participant beyond the setting its evidence originated in. A recipient may work in healthcare, manufacturing, construction, financial services, public administration or another sector.

**¶5.2**  If an entry is dominated by the technical details of one situation, a recipient may assume it is relevant only to a similar organization. A finance related disclosure may contain conduct relevant to other settings even where the financial issue is not.

**¶5.3**  Portability has five meanings that must not be conflated.

**Table 5.1. The five meanings of portability.**

| Serial | Meaning | What it asserts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Document portability | The record can be presented to an authorized recipient outside its original environment |
| 2 | Terminological consistency | Approved entries retain defined meanings across reports |
| 3 | Cross sector comprehensibility | Recipients from different industries can understand what the entries document |
| 4 | Contextual comparability | Relevant conditions across situations permit a common entry |
| 5 | Workplace transfer | Conduct documented in controlled situations generalizes or is maintained in actual employment |

**¶5.4**  A report may be portable as a document without establishing that the underlying situations were equivalent. It may be understandable across sectors without establishing relevance to every employer. It may document a repeated response without establishing that the person will respond the same way in employment.

**¶5.5**  Baldwin and Ford (1988) describe generalization and maintenance as central concerns in the transfer of training. Their analysis is a useful caution about claims concerning real workplace application. It does not establish that standardized entries are portable, equivalent across industries, or predictive.

> The report is intended to be portable as a governed evidence record while remaining bound to the situations, information, opportunities and observation modes from which its entries were derived.

### 5.1 Necessary context must survive abstraction

**¶5.6**  Removing detail can improve clarity. It can also remove a fact that materially changes interpretation. Authority, prompting, information availability, timing, opportunity and the participant's actual response may all matter.

**¶5.7**  An entry must not discard such information merely to make its wording more general. The architecture provides two mechanisms: **Recorded Contexts**, identifying the represented settings and modes, and **Differences Across Settings**, preserving material variation the common entry must not conceal.

**¶5.8**  These are the initial solution. A new narrative field is not automatically required and should be justified by testing that shows the existing fields insufficient.

> The design objective is to avoid unnecessary narrative detail without removing conditions that would materially change a reasonable reading of the evidence.

## 6. Protection against inferential expansion

**¶6.1**  Behavioral observations invite interpretation. A person identifies an ethical concern and an observer may be inclined to call the person ethical. A person acknowledges an error and an observer may be inclined to call the person accountable.

**¶6.2**  Those descriptions move from a documented event toward a broader characterization, and that transition requires additional assumptions. A response recorded in a defined situation does not by itself establish a stable personal disposition, nor does the presence of a response establish its recurrence in circumstances that were not observed.

**¶6.3**  This aligns with the validity literature's concern with the support required for interpretations drawn from observed responses (Messick, 1995; Kane, 2013). Those sources provide methodological principles. They do not establish the validity of this particular observation system.

> The 3rd Academy does not validate people. It governs evidence.

**¶6.4**  The report does not certify integrity, classify personality, establish employability or predict future conduct. Its authority rests on the provenance and support for particular recorded entries.

**¶6.5**  Factual support and permissible use are separate. An entry can accurately document what occurred while providing insufficient justification for a particular employment decision. Intended and prohibited uses are therefore specified separately from the rules that authorize entries.

## 7. Recurrence without concealing difference

**¶7.1**  A bounded system must not achieve consistency by erasing differences. The report separates the common supported entry from the contexts in which it was established and the material differences between them.

**¶7.2**  This matters most across observation modes. A written response may establish what a participant stated. A live interaction may establish what the participant communicated or did during that interaction. A group setting may involve a different authority relationship, sequence, opportunity to speak or response from other actors.

**¶7.3**  The common proposition must be supported by the evidence in each included context. Similarity of wording alone is insufficient.

### 7.1 The rule against retrospective broadening

**¶7.4**  A recurrence proposition must be defined **before** the relevant observations are compared, together with its admission conditions, material variables and contradiction rules.

**¶7.5**  The institution must not begin with different observations and progressively broaden the wording until every response appears recurrent.

**Table 7.1. Four propositions that are not interchangeable.**

| Serial | Candidate proposition | What it additionally requires |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The participant identified an issue | That an issue was identified |
| 2 | The participant communicated the existence of an issue | That it was communicated to someone |
| 3 | The participant initiated disclosure without prompting | That no prompt preceded it |
| 4 | The participant independently escalated an issue to an authorized person | That the recipient was authorized and the route was chosen |

**¶7.6**  A participant may satisfy 7.2 across several contexts without satisfying 7.3 or 7.4. A recurring entry must not claim more than the approved common proposition supports. Any substantive amendment requires a new controlled version and an impact review of affected records.

### 7.2 Recurrence and variation are complementary

**¶7.7**  The Recorded Conduct field expresses the approved common proposition. The Differences Across Settings field preserves meaningful variation relevant to its interpretation.

**¶7.8**  Where an issue was raised before questioning in one setting and after questioning in another, the report may document the common act of communication where supported, and must preserve the timing difference where that distinction is material.

> Recurrence does not erase difference, and difference does not necessarily negate recurrence of a narrower, independently supported act.

## 8. The evidence chain

**¶8.1**  The report functions as an institutional evidence record rather than a collection of impressions. That requires an auditable relationship between what occurred and what the report states.

**¶8.2**  **Source situation and opportunity → Evidence of participant response → Authorized human determination → Approved Recorded Conduct Entry → Report presentation**

**¶8.3**  Every transition introduces obligations.

**Table 8.1. The six transitions of the evidence chain and their obligations.**

| Serial | Transition | What must be established | Failure the control must prevent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Situation to genuine opportunity | The participant received the relevant information and an accessible opportunity to respond | Treating a missing response as an observed omission where no valid opportunity existed |
| 2 | Response to factual determination | The defined act, statement, prompt or timing relationship is supported by available evidence | Mistaking an observer's assumption for an established event |
| 3 | Determination to entry | The approved entry asserts no more than the determination and required context support | Converting "named someone" into "identified an authorized responsible person" |
| 4 | Multiple records to recurrence | A predefined common proposition is supported and material differences preserved | Broadening wording after seeing the records to manufacture consistency |
| 5 | Report to reader interpretation | Recipients understand the context and inferential limits | Reading stated intention as enacted conduct, or a recorded act as a personal trait |
| 6 | Interpretation to proposed use | The use is separately authorized and justified | Treating a historical observation as proof of future employment suitability |

**¶8.4**  These are proposed evidence obligations informed by argument based validation principles. They are not obligations already proven to have been satisfied.

### 8.1 Independent review must be described accurately

**¶8.5**  A source script establishes the situation that was designed or administered. It does not establish what the participant actually said or did.

**¶8.6**  Where the underlying response remains available for independent examination, a reviewer may reassess it within the approved process. Where only the original observer's governed determination survives, a second reviewer may examine that determination's documented basis and rule compliance. That review must not be described as independently verifying an underlying act that can no longer be independently established.

### 8.2 Agreement is not factual correctness

**¶8.7**  Two observers can agree and both be wrong. They may consistently withhold a supported entry because their training material is defective, or consistently admit an unsupported entry because a definition is too permissive. High agreement in either case would not establish factual adequacy.

**¶8.8**  Hallgren (2012) distinguishes inter rater reliability from the broader validity of an observational method.

**¶8.9**  The institution must therefore examine independent agreement on initial determinations, agreement with independently reviewed factual reference cases where such references can be established, unsupported admissions, supported entries incorrectly withheld, and the adequacy of material context and stated limitations.

## 9. The two opposite risks

### 9.1 What unbounded narrative would risk

**Table 9.1. Potential failure modes of unbounded narrative reporting.**

| Serial | Risk | Potential failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Observer dependent variation | Reports influenced by the observer's interpretive preferences rather than differences in evidence |
| 2 | Reduced calibration transparency | Harder to identify which disagreements concern facts and which concern style |
| 3 | Contamination by situational consequences | Comparable conduct described differently because the situation unfolded differently |
| 4 | Unsupported personal characterization | The report acquires interpretive authority its evidence does not support |
| 5 | Reduced cross sector comprehensibility | A recipient discounts relevant conduct because the situation is unfamiliar |
| 6 | Unequal treatment through irrelevant presentation features | Accent, fluency, cultural style or polish influence report language |
| 7 | More complex correction and challenge | Harder to identify which assertion is disputed and what supports it |
| 8 | Increased production and maintenance | Governance requirements increase before value is demonstrated |
| 9 | Employer overinterpretation | Recipients place more weight on the report than its evidence justifies |
| 10 | Erosion of the evidence governance distinction | The product resembles a subjective reference or behavioral appraisal |

**¶9.1**  These are plausible failure modes, not inevitable consequences of all qualitative reporting. Carefully governed narrative reporting can also be accurate, reliable and useful. Any future comparative claim would require testing against a credible, well governed alternative rather than an intentionally weak narrative format.

### 9.2 The opposite failure: entries that become too general

**¶9.2**  An overly broad entry may be consistently applied while communicating almost nothing. "The participant responded to a workplace situation" is easy to reproduce and nearly useless. An entry combining materially different acts under one category may conceal distinctions necessary for interpretation.

**¶9.3**  The solution is not unrestricted narrative expansion. It is a disciplined balance between portability and evidentiary specificity, enforced by the two tests at Section 31.

> Abstraction is justified only while the entry continues to preserve the facts necessary for correct interpretation.

**¶9.4**  The institution should monitor both unsupported admissions and supported entries incorrectly withheld. A highly restrictive library may avoid overstatement while producing too little evidence. A highly permissive library may produce many entries while weakening their factual support. The objective is neither maximum admission nor maximum withholding. It is warranted admission under the approved evidence rules.

## 10. Why the minimum viable report begins here

**¶10.1**  The first report supports proof of concept. The institution must establish that its observation pathway produces governed records, that authorized human determinations can be made consistently, and that the reporting system renders supported entries accurately.

**¶10.2**  The commercial value of additional narrative detail is unknown. Substantial expansion before the pilot would require more authors, reviewers, calibration material, development work and maintenance.

**¶10.3**  The central operating proposition is:

> Can defined observations be converted into traceable, bounded, participant reviewable evidence without unsupported inference?

> A minimum viable report should minimize unverified interpretive complexity, not minimize scientific discipline.

## 11. The long term development principle

**¶11.1**  The library is not intended to freeze the evidence vocabulary permanently. Future evidence may justify narrower distinctions, additional propositions, improved contextual qualifiers or better presentation.

**¶11.2**  Every proposed expansion must pass four tests, applied at Section 43.

**Table 11.1. The four expansion tests.**

| Serial | Test | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evidentiary necessity | Does the addition capture a meaningful distinction established by the governed source evidence? |
| 2 | Calibration feasibility | Can independent authorized observers apply the proposed entry and its admission conditions consistently? |
| 3 | Participant fairness | Does the addition preserve transparency, challenge rights, and protection against unsupported characterization? |
| 4 | Demonstrated usefulness | Does the added complexity respond to an identified need of the report's intended users? |

**¶11.3**  Learning must occur through versioned, documented change rather than uncontrolled observer discretion. The original version, applicable evidence conditions and reasons for revision must remain traceable.

## 12. Institutional position

> Given the same admissible evidence, governing rules and library version, independent authorized observers should reach the same defensible recorded conduct determination, or document why the evidence does not permit that result.

**¶12.1**  Where the evidence differs materially, the determination or contextual qualification may differ. Where the evidence is inadequate, the entry must be withheld. Where an original act cannot be independently re examined, the institution must acknowledge that limitation. Where a recipient proposes a consequential use, the appropriateness of that use must be evaluated separately from the factual support for the entry.

**¶12.2**  These boundaries do not diminish the report. They define the scope of its institutional authority.

> The objective is not to produce the most elaborate description of a participant. It is to produce a record whose entries, provenance, supporting conditions and limitations can be examined, understood, reproduced where the evidence permits, and challenged through governed procedures.

# PART B — WHAT A RECORDED CONDUCT ENTRY IS

## 13. Vocabulary

**Table 13.1. Controlled vocabulary of this Standard.**

| Serial | Term | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recorded Conduct Entry | The catalogue item. One approved wording expressing one governed determination. Abbreviated RCE in discussion, never in a document |
| 2 | Clause | How an entry behaves when composed. An entry is a clause, not a sentence |
| 3 | Recorded Conduct Library | The complete set of approved entries for one dimension |
| 4 | Composed text | The field holding the entry's approved wording |
| 5 | Bounded recorded conduct | The approach. Not the artifact |

**¶13.1**  **The relationship between the approach and the artifact.** Bounded recorded conduct is the approach. The Recorded Conduct Entry is the artifact that expresses it. Both terms are current and neither supersedes the other. Where an earlier governing document says "bounded recorded conduct statement," it refers to what this Standard calls a Recorded Conduct Entry.

**¶13.2**  **The identifier prefix.** Every entry carries an identifier of the form `ST-Dn-nnn`. The prefix `ST-` is retained as an opaque code. It is not an abbreviation of anything in current use and must not be expanded. Retaining it means no published library requires renumbering.

**Table 13.2. Mapping from prior terms.**

| Serial | Prior term | Current term |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Statement | Recorded Conduct Entry |
| 2 | Statement library | Recorded Conduct Library |
| 3 | Approved statement library entry | Recorded Conduct Entry |
| 4 | Bounded recorded conduct statement | Recorded Conduct Entry, expressing bounded recorded conduct |

## 14. What the entry is

**¶14.1**  A Recorded Conduct Entry is the authorized expression of a single governed determination, drawn from an approved library rather than written by the observer.

**¶14.2**  **The entry is not the determination.** The determination is the observer's accountable judgment that the available evidence supports a specified proposition. The entry is the controlled wording that expresses that judgment in the report.

**¶14.3**  This distinction is not academic. It is what makes amendment coherent. A version change to an entry alters **how a determination reads**, not **what was determined**. Without the distinction, Section 41 could not say whether a revision changes the record or only its wording.

## 15. What the entry asserts, and the two entry types

**¶15.1**  One bounded proposition, supported by the available evidence under the applicable rules.

**¶15.2**  One proposition, not a bundle. Where two facts must both be recorded, they are two entries or one entry explicitly composed of both, never one entry silently carrying two claims.

**¶15.3**  **There are two entry types and they are governed differently.**

**Table 15.1. The two entry types.**

| Serial | Type | Proposition is about | Grammatical subject | Enters a recurrence key |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Conduct bearing | An act or statement of the participant | The participant, or a fixed artifact under the exception at Section 31.3 | Yes |
| 2 | Record status | The state of the record itself | The thing not observed | **Never** |

**¶15.4**  **A record status entry is the only permitted exception to the subject test at Section 31.2.** It exists because every answer set terminates in an answer meaning the thing was not observed, and that answer needs an entry. Without the exception a question facing an absence would have no admissible entry, and an observer would be forced into a conduct bearing entry that the record cannot support.

**¶15.5**  **The limits of the exception.** A record status entry asserts an absence in the record, never an absence in the participant. It carries no implication that the act did not occur. It never enters a recurrence key, because an absence in the record is not conduct and a repeated absence is not repeated conduct. It is written in the form *no X was observed within the recorded period*, and never in the form *the participant did not X*.

**¶15.6**  **Which type an entry is, is a property of the entry and is recorded on it.** See Section 19.

## 16. What the entry does not assert, and must not

**Table 16.1. Assertions an entry must never carry.**

| Serial | Prohibited assertion | Example of the failure |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A personal attribute | the participant is honest |
| 2 | A tendency or disposition | the participant tends to raise concerns |
| 3 | A prediction | the participant would raise a comparable issue in employment |
| 4 | A judgment of the act | the participant appropriately raised the issue |
| 5 | A consequence of the act | the participant's disclosure prevented a loss |
| 6 | A state of mind or manner | the participant confidently raised the issue |

**¶16.1**  **This is a refusal, not a preference.** An entry asserting any of the above must not be authored. Where one is found in a published library, it is recorded in the Standing Defects Register at Section 47 and withdrawn or revised under Section 42.

## 17. Where the entry sits

**¶17.1**  The entry occupies exactly one transition in the evidence chain at Section 8: **determination to entry**, row 8.3.

**Table 17.1. Obligations on either side of the entry.**

| Serial | Side | Obligation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Behind the entry | The determination must be supported by available evidence, made by an authorized observer, and recorded with its basis |
| 2 | The entry itself | Must assert no more than the determination and its required context support |
| 3 | In front of the entry | The report must present it with its recorded contexts and material differences, so a reader understands its limits |

**¶17.2**  The entry carries no obligation that belongs behind or in front of it. It does not have to prove the determination and it does not have to prevent every misreading. It has to express the determination and nothing more.

## 18. How the entry behaves

**¶18.1**  **An entry is a clause, not a sentence.** It is never rendered alone as a capitalized standalone sentence about a person.

**¶18.2**  **The grammatical subject is the participant, with two defined exceptions.** An artifact comparison entry under Section 31.4 takes the artifact as its subject. A record status entry under Table 15.1 takes the thing not observed. Both are governed; neither is a departure an author may make at will.

**¶18.3**  **The composition contract.** The entry carries its own grammatical subject, which is the participant. The report frame supplies the contextual lead in that binds the entry to its recorded contexts, and the frame's lead in ends in a colon or an equivalent connective so that the entry continues it. The entry does not supply the lead in and the frame does not supply the subject.

**Table 18.1. The composition contract.**

| Serial | Supplied by | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 18.0a | The report frame | The contextual lead in naming the recorded contexts the entry is established in |
| 18.0b | The entry | The grammatical subject, the verb, and the proposition |
| 18.0c | The report frame | The adjacent fields: recorded contexts, and differences across settings |

**¶18.4**  Four rules follow, and they apply to every entry in every dimension.

**Table 18.2. The four composition rules.**

| Serial | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | Stored beginning in lower case |
| 2 | Begins mid sentence, as a continuation rather than an opening |
| 3 | Carries no terminal capitalization or framing of its own |
| 4 | Is uniform in these respects across the whole library |

**¶18.5**  **Worked check against D1.** Forty two of the forty four D1 entries comply. Two do not: ST-D1-083 begins "The disclosure followed a direct question about the interest," and ST-D1-083b begins "The disclosure was made before any direct question." Both carry an initial capital where their forty two siblings do not. Composed into a frame, both produce a capital mid sentence. Both are recorded at Section 47.

## 19. Anatomy of an entry

**¶19.1**  Every entry carries the following. An entry missing any of these may not enter a production approval path.

**Table 19.1. The thirteen required properties of a Recorded Conduct Entry.**

| Serial | Property | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identifier | Conforms to the grammar at Section 20. Unique within the dimension, never reused |
| 19.1a | Entry type | Conduct bearing or record status, per Table 15.1 |
| 2 | Question and answer mapping | Exactly one question object and exactly one answer within it |
| 3 | Composed text | The approved wording, complying with Section 18 |
| 4 | Bound placeholders | Named placeholders the source fills at render, or none |
| 5 | Recurrence key | The key this entry may enter, or an explicit exclusion |
| 6 | Defined referent | The factual or behavioral thing the entry describes |
| 7 | Admission conditions | What the evidence must establish for the entry to be admitted |
| 8 | Withholding conditions | What causes the entry to be withheld |
| 9 | Material variable rules | Rules governing opportunities, prompts, information and timing |
| 10 | Scope of permissible interpretation | What a reader may and may not take from it |
| 11 | Version history | Controlled, with reasons for each revision |
| 12 | Calibration evidence | Testing appropriate to intended use, or an explicit statement that none exists yet |

**¶19.2**  Properties 19.6 through 19.12 derive from the institutional rationale at Section 9.2 and are reproduced here as requirements rather than as principles.

**¶19.3**  **Current state, stated plainly.** In the published D1, D2, D3, D4, D5 and D8 libraries, properties 19.1 through 19.5 are populated. Properties 19.6 through 19.10 are held in the dimension's construct and source sheets rather than on the entry row. 19.11 exists at document level, not entry level. 19.12 is empty across every dimension because the pilot has not run. ## 20. Identifier grammar

**¶20.1**  An identifier takes the form `ST-Dn-nnn` optionally followed by a single lower case suffix letter.

**Table 20.1. Identifier grammar.**

| Serial | Element | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prefix | `ST-`, retained as an opaque code, never expanded |
| 2 | Dimension | `Dn` or `Dnn`, matching the dimension number |
| 3 | Sequence | Three digits. The first two indicate the question object; the third the answer within it |
| 4 | Suffix | A single lower case letter, permitted where one answer resolves to more than one entry, or where an entry was added between existing sequence numbers |

**¶20.2**  A suffix is not a version marker and never indicates a revision. `ST-D1-054a` and `ST-D1-054b` are two distinct entries serving two distinct child questions. `ST-D1-083b` and `ST-D1-083c` are entries added within an existing sequence. Both patterns are valid and neither requires renumbering.

## 21. A worked entry

**¶21.1**  ST-D1-011, taken apart against Sections B2 to B7.

> **Composed text:** the participant stated that a specific issue existed and identified what it was.

**Table 21.1. ST-D1-011 against the anatomy.**

| Serial | Property | Value for ST-D1-011 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identifier | ST-D1-011 |
| 2 | Question | Q-D1-01, identification of the issue |
| 3 | Answer | Named the specific issue |
| 4 | Sibling entries | ST-D1-012 referred without identifying; ST-D1-013 no reference observed |
| 5 | Bound placeholders | None |
| 6 | Recurrence key | REK-D1-001, whether the participant identified what the specific issue was |
| 7 | Opposite pair | ST-D1-011 against ST-D1-012 |

**¶21.2**  **What it asserts.** That the participant made a statement, and that the statement identified a particular issue rather than gesturing at one. Two components of one proposition about one act.

**¶21.3**  **What it does not assert.** That the issue was real. That identifying it was correct, courageous, or timely. That anyone acted on it. That the participant would do so again. Any of these would breach Section 16.

**¶21.4**  **Why the issue itself is not named.** This is the property most often misread as vagueness. The entry asserts that a specific issue was identified without reproducing which. Naming it would bind the entry to one fictional situation, defeating cross sector comprehensibility and contextual comparability, rows 5.3 and 5.4 of Table 5.1. **The specificity is claimed and bounded, not supplied.**

**¶21.5**  **Where the specific does appear, the source supplies it.** Two D1 entries carry bound placeholders: ST-D1-032 carries `{omitted_items}` and ST-D1-061 carries `{route_used}`. The entry stays portable; the instance carries the particular. See Section 36.

**¶21.6**  **How it behaves in composition.** Lower case, mid sentence, no framing of its own. It composes into the report frame, which supplies the subject and the recorded context bound. Rendered alone as a capitalized standalone sentence, it reads as a finding about a person rather than one recorded act, which is the failure Section 18 exists to prevent.

# PART C — THE REPORT FACE

**¶21.7**  Part C governs where a Recorded Conduct Entry lands. It walks the Behavioral Evidence Report element by element, in the order the reader meets them, and states for each what it says, why it is there, and what would go wrong without it.

**¶21.8**  An author who knows what an entry is but not where it appears will write entries that are correct in isolation and wrong in place. That is the failure this Part prevents.

## 22. What the report face is for

**¶22.1**  The Behavioral Evidence Report is read by someone who was not present at the observation, who did not author the sources, and who has an employment decision in front of them. Its structure is not a layout preference. Every element on it answers a question that reader would otherwise answer for themselves, and answer wrongly.

**¶22.2**  **Why the report is structured as it is.** A reader given only a finding will supply the rest. Shown that a participant identified an issue, they will conclude that the participant is someone who identifies issues, that this will hold in their workplace, and that the report is recommending them. None of those follow from the evidence. **The structure exists to answer each of those questions on the page, before the reader answers it in their head.** That is why the limits are distributed rather than collected, why the entry is set apart from its framing, why absence is stated in words rather than shown as a gap, and why the one prohibition on use appears in plain language rather than in a disclaimer.

**¶22.3**  **The second reason is institutional rather than interpretive.** A record that cannot be checked, dated, traced to a version, or withdrawn by the person it concerns is not evidence; it is an opinion in circulation. The identification block, the verification reference, the currency date and the participant control line exist so that the document can be tested rather than trusted.

**¶22.4**  **The third reason is that the report must survive being read in isolation.** It will reach people who have never seen this Standard, the Execution Editions, or any explanation of the system. Everything the institution can supply to make it readable is on its face, which is why the relationship between recurrence and difference is stated on the page rather than assumed. **Whether that is sufficient is tested, not asserted.**

**¶22.5**  The Behavioral Evidence Report is read by someone who was not present at the observation, who did not author the sources, and who has an employment decision in front of them. Every element on the face exists to make one of three things true for that reader: they understand what the report says, they understand what it does not say, and they can check that the copy in their hand is current.

**Table 22.1. The three purposes of the report face.**

| Serial | Purpose | Elements serving it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The reader understands what the report says | Recorded Conduct table, Differences Across Settings, how differences are shown |
| 2 | The reader understands what it does not say | Scope and limits, the limiting paragraph, dimensions not represented, permitted use, disclosure limits |
| 3 | The reader can check the copy | Report identification, verification reference, evidence currency date, availability and status |

### 22.1 The design rule

**¶22.6**  No element may serve the first purpose at the cost of the second. Where a presentation choice would make the report clearer and simultaneously invite a reader to take more from it than the evidence supports, the limiting purpose wins.

### 22.4 What item 10 does, and the comparison that clarifies it

**¶22.12**  Item 10 is the clearest expression of the whole design, and it is worth stating in the terms a reader outside the institution would use.

**¶22.13**  **A familiar comparison, with its limits.** A language proficiency report, a driving examination result, or a trade certification record all share one property with the Behavioral Evidence Report: **each states what was recorded on a defined occasion, and none of them states how the person will perform on the next one.** A language report does not assert how the holder will speak in a meeting six months later. It records what was recorded, under stated conditions, on a stated date.

**¶22.14**  That shared property is exactly what item 10 makes explicit on this face.

**¶22.15**  **Where the comparison stops, and this matters.** Those instruments produce a result on a scale, and comparison between holders is part of their purpose. **The Behavioral Evidence Report produces no scale, no result, no rank and no comparison between participants.** It records whether specified conduct was supported by the available evidence, and nothing more. The comparison is therefore useful for explaining the limit on inference and must not be extended to the form of the output.

**Table 22.4. What the comparison establishes and where it stops.**

| Serial | Property | Shared with proficiency and certification reports | Not shared |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Records what occurred on a defined occasion | Shared | |
| 2 | States the conditions under which it was recorded | Shared | |
| 3 | Carries a date beyond which it should not be relied on | Shared | |
| 4 | Makes no claim about future performance | Shared | |
| 5 | Produces a score or a scale position | | Not shared |
| 6 | Supports comparison between holders | | Not shared |
| 7 | Supports a pass or fail determination | | Not shared |

**¶22.16**  **The sentence item 10 exists to prevent.** Without it, a reader finishing item 9 would complete the thought themselves: *this is someone who raises issues.* That sentence is about a person rather than an occasion, and nothing in the evidence supports it. Item 10 says so on the page, next to the entry, before the reader gets there.

## 23. Header and identification

**¶23.1**  **What appears.** The institution name, the report title, the purpose line, the participant name, a status marker, the report identifier and version, the issue date, the evidence currency date, the verification address, and the employer visibility line.

**Table 23.1. The six header elements and their purpose.**

| Serial | Element | Why it is there | Without it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Purpose line beneath the title | States in one sentence that the report documents selected conduct in defined situations, and that the participant controls availability | A reader assumes the report is comprehensive and institution issued without participant consent |
| 2 | Report identifier and version | Makes one specific rendering citable and distinguishable from a later one | Two renderings of the same participant cannot be told apart, and an amendment cannot be traced |
| 3 | Issue date | Fixes when the evidence was assembled | A reader cannot tell whether the content predates a correction |
| 4 | Evidence currency date | States the date beyond which the report must not be relied on | An old report circulates indefinitely and is read as current |
| 5 | Verification address | Lets a reader confirm authenticity and current status independently of the copy they hold | A forged or superseded copy cannot be distinguished from a current one |
| 6 | Employer visibility line | States that availability is participant controlled and may be withdrawn | The report reads as institutionally published about a person rather than released by them |

**¶23.2**  **Why the evidence currency date is separate from the issue date.** The issue date says when the report was made. The currency date says when it stops being reliable. Collapsing them into one would leave a reader to decide for themselves how old is too old, which is a judgment the institution must make rather than delegate.

**¶23.3**  **What the verification check must not disclose.** It confirms authenticity and current status. It does not disclose report content or participant identity. A verification route that disclosed content would become a way to read a report without the participant's release.

## 24. Scope and limits

**¶24.1**  **What appears.** A paragraph above the Recorded Conduct table stating that the report contains recorded conduct for represented dimensions only, that it is not a complete account, that dimensions not represented are listed, that their absence is not a conclusion, and that the report does not state whether a participant should be selected, placed, promoted or preferred.

**¶24.2**  **Why it sits above the table rather than below.** A reader meets the limits before the content. A limiting paragraph placed after the table is read, if at all, by someone who has already formed an impression.

**¶24.3**  **Why absence is addressed twice.** The paragraph states that absence is not a conclusion, and Section 28 lists what is absent. The first is the rule; the second is the fact. A reader given the fact without the rule infers a gap. A reader given the rule without the fact cannot tell what is missing.

**¶24.4**  **The sentence that does the most work.** *This report records selected conduct in defined situations and does not state whether a participant should be selected, placed, promoted, preferred, or otherwise chosen for an opportunity.* It is the only place on the face where the report declines a use in plain words rather than describing its own content.

## 25. The Recorded Conduct table

**¶25.1**  Four columns. Each carries a different kind of thing and the separation is load bearing.

**Table 25.1. The four columns and what each may carry.**

| Serial | Column | Carries | Must never carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dimension | The dimension identifier and name | A rank, a score, or an ordering implying importance |
| 2 | Recorded Contexts | Which Stage contexts the evidence comes from, and what each Stage is | A judgment that one context is stronger than another |
| 3 | Observed Conduct | The Recorded Conduct Entry, composed into its frame | A narrative, a summary, or anything not drawn from the approved library |
| 4 | Differences Across Settings | Material variation the common entry would otherwise conceal | A resolution of the difference, or a preference between settings |

**¶25.2**  **Why Recorded Contexts names the Stage in words.** A reader who sees only S1, S2 and S4 cannot tell what kind of evidence underlies the entry. Naming them — written situation, live interaction, group setting — tells a reader that the entry rests on three different modes of observation, which is material to how much it supports.

**¶25.3**  **Why the dimension column carries no ordering.** Dimensions appear in their identifier order, which is a catalogue order and not a priority. Any other order would imply the institution has ranked them.

**¶25.4**  **Only dimensions with a record appear in the table.** A dimension with no record is not given an empty row. Empty rows read as pending or as a gap in the participant, and neither is what the absence means. Absence is recorded once, in controlled language, at Section 28.

**¶25.5**  **The row count and the not represented list must reconcile.** The number of dimensions in the table plus the number in the not represented list equals fourteen, always. A reader who counts and finds otherwise has grounds to doubt everything else on the page.

## 26. The entry in place

**¶26.1**  **What appears.** A label naming the evidence state, a lead in binding the entry to its recorded contexts, the entry itself, and a limiting paragraph beneath it.

**¶26.2**  **The composition contract in practice.** The frame supplies the lead in. The entry supplies the subject and the proposition. Section 18 governs this and the report face must not depart from it.

**Table 26.1. The four elements surrounding an entry on the face.**

| Serial | Element | Example from the sample layout |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evidence state label | BOUNDED RECURRENCE |
| 2 | Frame lead in | Recorded in each of the three Stage contexts identified in this report: |
| 3 | The entry | the participant stated that a specific issue existed and identified what it was |
| 4 | Limiting paragraph | This statement is limited to the recorded contexts. It is not a statement about character, general tendency, suitability, or future behavior |

**¶26.3**  **Why the evidence state is labelled.** An entry established in one context and an entry established in three are different claims. Without the label a reader cannot tell which they are reading, and will tend to read every entry as the stronger one.

**¶26.4**  **Why the limiting paragraph is adjacent to the entry rather than collected at the foot of the page.** It names the four things the entry is not: character, general tendency, suitability, future behavior. Those are the four inferences a reader is most likely to draw, and they are drawn at the moment of reading the entry, not at the end of the page.

**¶26.5**  **Why the entry is rendered with a visual boundary.** The entry is the only text on the face drawn from the approved library. Everything around it is framing the institution wrote. Setting the entry apart tells a reader which is which.

## 27. Differences across settings

**¶27.1**  **What appears.** The material variation between the recorded contexts, stated per context, followed by a statement that the observations remain separate.

**¶27.2**  **The three rules this field enforces.**

**Table 27.1. The three rules of the differences field.**

| Serial | Rule | What it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Neither context is preferred | The report implying that one mode of observation counts for more |
| 2 | Contexts are not averaged | A difference being dissolved into a single weaker claim |
| 3 | The difference is not used to broaden the recurrence statement | Retrospective broadening, prohibited at Section 44 |

**¶27.3**  **Why this field exists at all.** It is what lets an entry be general without being thin. The entry carries what is common; this field carries what differs. Without it an author must either cram every material fact into the entry, making it unportable, or discard the variation, making the report misleading. Section 38 governs the authoring side of the same principle.

**¶27.4**  **The footer that states the relationship.** *A difference does not erase recurrence of a separate clause, and recurrence does not erase the difference.* This sentence is the whole design of the two fields in one line, and it appears on the face because a reader who does not understand the relationship will read the difference as undermining the entry.

## 28. Dimensions not represented

**¶28.1**  **What appears.** A statement that no observation was scheduled or completed for the dimensions listed, or that they were outside the activated scope, that no result exists for them, and that their absence is not a conclusion about the participant. Then the list, naming each dimension in full.

**¶28.2**  **Why the dimensions are named rather than counted.** A reader told that eight dimensions are unrepresented learns nothing about which. A reader given the names can see that the report covers what it covers and no more.

**¶28.3**  **Why two causes are stated.** Not scheduled or completed, and outside the activated scope, are different things. The first means an observation could have happened and did not. The second means it was never in scope. Collapsing them would leave a reader to assume the first in every case, which reads as something the participant failed to do.

**¶28.4**  **Why absence is not a conclusion, stated explicitly.** This is the inference the list most invites. A list of thirteen unrepresented dimensions beside one represented dimension reads, without the sentence, as a report of extensive gaps.

## 29. The closing blocks

**¶29.1**  Five blocks follow the table. Each addresses a different reader question.

**Table 29.1. The five closing blocks.**

| Serial | Block | The question it answers | What it must state |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Participant review | Did the participant see this before it was issued? | That review occurred before issuance, and whether any correction is open |
| 2 | Availability and status | How did I come to be reading this, and for how long is it good? | That availability is participant controlled, the currency date, and the verification reference |
| 3 | Evidence note | How was the evidence produced? | That Stage 1 is administered through an artificial intelligence system, and that a Stage 1 observation enters the record only after authorized human confirmation |
| 4 | Permitted use | What may I do with this? | That it is supplementary, may be one source among others, does not recommend a decision, must not be the sole basis for adverse action, and is not a prediction |
| 5 | Disclosure limits and record integrity | What am I not seeing? | That it covers one evidence window, that withdrawal ends future availability but does not recall a copy already read, and that earlier or later reports may exist |

**¶29.2**  **Why the evidence note names the artificial intelligence administration explicitly.** A reader who learns later that part of the observation was machine administered, and who was not told, will discount the whole report. Stating it, together with the human confirmation requirement, is what makes the disclosure a strength rather than a discovered weakness.

**¶29.3**  **Why permitted use states a prohibition and not only a permission.** *Must not be used as the sole basis for adverse employment action* is the one sentence on the face that constrains the reader rather than describing the report. It is the practical expression of the separation at Section 6 between factual support and permissible use.

**¶29.4**  **Why disclosure limits say that withdrawal does not recall a copy already read.** It would be easy to imply that participant control is absolute. It is not. Saying so protects the participant from a false expectation and the institution from a claim it cannot meet.

## 31. The governing tests

**¶31.1**  These are applied throughout, not at the end. Every entry must pass both.

### 31.1 The scope test

> An entry is correctly bounded when its defined meaning and admission conditions remain stable across **every** approved context in which it may be used, and the entry together with its required context retains the material facts needed to interpret the act.

**¶31.2**  **The quantifier is universal, not existential.** It is not enough that some other industry exists in which the wording happens to be accurate. The entry must hold across all approved contexts carrying the defined proposition and its material conditions. An earlier form of this test asked only whether the wording would be accurate in a different industry; that form admitted an entry on the strength of one convenient case.

**¶31.3**  **A particular is not refused merely for being situation specific.** It is included, or bound to a placeholder under Section 37, where omitting it would materially alter the proposition. Portability is not a reason to discard a fact the proposition depends on.

**¶31.4**  Failing the first half means the entry is **too specific**. Failing the second means it is **too general**.

**Table 31.1. The scope test applied to three candidate wordings.**

| Serial | Candidate wording | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | the participant stated the invoice to the client organization overstated hours by eleven | Too specific. Accurate only in that situation |
| 2 | the participant responded to the situation | Too general. Tells a reader nothing about what was done |
| 3 | the participant stated that a specific issue existed and identified what it was | Passes both halves |

### 31.2 The subject test

> The grammatical subject is the participant, and the verb is an act of theirs.

**¶31.5**  An entry whose subject is the observer, the source, the evidence, or a correspondence between them has put a determination inside the wording instead of behind it.

**¶31.6**  **Worked failure from D1.** ST-D1-045 reads: *the participant used responsibility language about another person that mapped to the source's pre-defined support set.* The subject is the participant, but the proposition is a mapping the observer established, not an act the participant performed. Its three siblings all name what the participant did.

**Table 31.2. The subject test applied to the Q-D1-04b entries.**

| Serial | Entry | Proposition | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ST-D1-044 | made no statement attributing responsibility | Act. Passes |
| 2 | ST-D1-045 | used language that mapped to a support set | Mapping. Fails |
| 3 | ST-D1-046 | attributed responsibility in terms the source does not provide | Act. Passes |
| 4 | ST-D1-047 | attributed responsibility, and one or more did not correspond | Act. Passes |

**¶31.7**  ST-D1-045 would pass the scope test and fail the subject test. That is why both tests exist. It is recorded at Section 47.

### 31.3 The assertion decomposition test

**¶31.8**  **A permitted verb does not authorize its object.** The subject test inspects the grammatical subject and the verb. It does not inspect what the rest of the clause asserts or presupposes, and an entry can pass both earlier tests while asserting more than the determination supports.

**¶31.9**  **Worked failure.** A participant says: *it might be a duplicate entry, I have not checked.* An author writes:

> the participant identified the cause of the discrepancy

**¶31.10**  Subject is the participant. *Identified* is on the permitted verb list at Section 36. The wording is stable across approved contexts and tells a reader what was done. **All three earlier checks pass and the entry is wrong.** The overstatement sits in the definite description *the cause*, which asserts that a cause was established and that this was it. The response establishes that a possible explanation was offered.

**¶31.11**  **The test.**

> For every entry, list each fact the complete wording asserts or presupposes, and state the evidence each one requires. Permitted verbs do not authorize unsupported objects, complements, definite descriptions or presuppositions.

| Serial | Fact asserted or presupposed | Evidence required | Established |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A discrepancy existed | The source establishes it | Yes |
| 2 | That discrepancy has a cause | Follows from 1 | Yes |
| 3 | The cause was established | A determination that it was | **No** |
| 4 | The participant was the one who established it | As above | **No** |

**Table 31.3. Assertion decomposition of a failing entry.**

**¶31.12**  Rows 3 and 4 have no evidence, so the entry is refused. The supported wording is:

> the participant proposed a possible explanation for the discrepancy

**¶31.13**  **The decomposition tests what is asserted. It does not test how it was said.**

> Before composing, record the factual status of the underlying response and confirm the entry preserves it. An entry must not assert more certainty, more completion or more directness than the response establishes.

| Serial | Axis | The response was | The entry must not say |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Modality | Qualified: *might be*, *possibly*, *I think* | That the thing was so |
| 2 | Actuality | Hypothetical or conditional: *if that happened, I would* | That it happened or was done |
| 3 | Attribution | A quotation or a report of someone else's words | That the participant asserted it |
| 4 | Completion | A stated intention: *I will raise it* | That it was raised |
| 5 | Directness | A response to a prompt | That it was volunteered |

**Table 31.4. The five factual status axes.**

**¶31.14**  **Worked failure.** A participant says *there might be a duplicate charge in the invoice.* The entry *the participant stated that a duplicate charge existed and identified it* is portable, has the participant as subject, uses an approved verb and carries no judgment. **It fails axis 1.** The qualification was removed and the entry asserts a fact the response offered as a possibility.

**¶31.15**  **Axes 4 and 5 are the ones most often lost.** An intention composed as an act, and a prompted response composed as an unprompted one, are the two conversions this estate has already had to correct in source banks.

**¶31.16**  **Where presuppositions hide.** Definite descriptions (*the cause*, *the error*, *the correct figure*), factive verbs (*recognized*, *realized*, *noticed*), and comparatives all carry assertions beyond the verb. An author who cannot name the evidence for each row of the decomposition has not finished the entry.

### 31.4 The artifact comparison exception

**¶31.17**  An entry may record a comparison between two fixed artifacts without failing the subject test, provided every condition below is met.

**Table 31.5. The four conditions of the artifact comparison exception.**

| Serial | Condition |
|---|---|
| 1 | Both things compared are fixed artifacts: a record the participant produced, and a reference the source established before the observation |
| 2 | A reader holding both artifacts can check the comparison without being present at the observation |
| 3 | The grammatical subject is the artifact, not the participant performing the comparison |
| 4 | The entry names the reference it was compared against |

**¶31.18**  **Why ST-D1-045 fails the exception and ST-D1-031 passes it.** ST-D1-031 reads: *the account the participant gave was consistent with the source provided information identified for this observation.* Its subject is the account. Both artifacts are fixed. A reader holding the account and the source information can check it. It names the reference.

**¶31.19**  ST-D1-045 reads: *the participant used responsibility language about another person that mapped to the source's pre-defined support set.* Its subject is the participant, and the verb construction attributes the mapping to them, as though the participant performed a comparison against a set they never saw. The mapping was performed by the observer.

**Table 31.6. The artifact comparison exception applied to six D1 entries.**

| Serial | Entry | Subject | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ST-D1-031 | the account | Passes under the exception |
| 2 | ST-D1-032 | the account | Passes under the exception |
| 3 | ST-D1-033 | the account | Passes under the exception |
| 4 | ST-D1-034 | the account | Passes under the exception |
| 5 | ST-D1-071 | the second account | Passes under the exception |
| 6 | ST-D1-045 | the participant, performing a mapping | Fails. Recorded at Section 47 |

**¶31.20**  An entry that compares the participant's conduct against a standard, an expectation, or a judgment of adequacy is **not** an artifact comparison and is refused under Table 16.1.

**¶31.21**  **Eligibility is a property of the served instance, not of the identifier.** Whether both things compared are fixed artifacts depends on what was preserved in that instance. A written response retained in the record is a fixed artifact. **An account given live and not preserved is not**, and an observer's later reconstruction of it must never be substituted for one: that would check the observer's determination against the observer's own reconstruction. Where either artifact is unavailable for the instance, this exception does not authorize the entry.

### 31.5 The comparison operator

**¶31.22**  **Two fixed artifacts do not make a comparison testable.** The four conditions establish what may be compared. They say nothing about **how**, and an undefined relation leaves two observers applying different standards while both following the rules.

**¶31.23**  **Worked failure.** A source establishes three discrepancies. The participant's account says only *a review took place*. Against the entry *the account was consistent with the source provided information*: both artifacts are fixed, a reader can inspect both, the reference is named, the subject is the artifact. **All four conditions pass and nothing material was represented.** The account contradicts nothing and conveys nothing.

> Every artifact comparison entry declares its comparison operator. The operator states the relation tested, the domain over which it is tested, and the decision rule a reviewer applies. An entry whose operator is undeclared is refused.

| Serial | Operator | Tests | Satisfied when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Non contradiction | Whether the account asserts anything the reference excludes | No asserted item conflicts with the reference |
| 2 | Correspondence | Whether each item the account asserts matches the reference | Every asserted item matches |
| 3 | Coverage | Whether the account addresses the items the reference designates | Every designated item appears |
| 4 | Omission | Which designated items the account does not address | **Returns a typed result**: the enumerated designated items not addressed, or `unresolved` where the comparison cannot be established. It is not a pass or fail |

**Table 31.7. The four comparison operators.**

**¶31.24**  **Three of the four operators return a verdict. Omission returns a list.** Operators 1, 2 and 3 are satisfied or not. Operator 4 answers a different kind of question and returns the items, which may be none.

**¶31.25**  **The failing example resolved.**

| Serial | Operator | Result on the example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Non contradiction | **Satisfied.** The account asserts nothing the reference excludes |
| 2 | Correspondence | **Not established.** It requires checking what the account asserts against the reference, and absence of contradiction does not establish it |
| 3 | Coverage | **Fails.** None of the three designated discrepancies is addressed |
| 4 | Omission | **Returns the three designated discrepancies not addressed** |

**Table 31.8. The four operators applied to the failing example.**

**¶31.26**  **Detecting an omission successfully is not the same as the account satisfying coverage.** Operator 4 did its job; the account failed. An earlier form of this Standard conflated the two and described operator 4 as failing, which would have rejected the operation its own definition permits. **An entry asserting an omission requires a supported, non empty result from operator 4.**

**¶31.27**  **These are different relations and must never be reported in the same words.** *Consistent* is therefore not an admissible entry wording unless the entry names which operator it rests on and the source designates the domain.

**¶31.28**  **The domain must be designated before the observation.** An operator applied to a domain chosen after reading the account is the retrospective broadening prohibition at Section 44 in another form.

**¶31.29**  **Positive and negative correspondence are governed identically.** An entry may qualify an act against a named reference whether the correspondence holds, fails, or is mixed. Polarity is not what distinguishes a permitted qualification from a refused one.

| Serial | Construction | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The act is predicated of the participant and qualified against a named reference | Permitted under the qualification conditions at Section 31.6 |
| 2 | The comparison operation itself is predicated of the participant | **Refused.** The participant did not perform the comparison |

**Table 31.9. What distinguishes a permitted qualification from a refused one.**

**¶31.30**  ST-D1-046 reads *attributed responsibility to another person in terms the source information does not provide*. The act is the participant's; the comparison qualifies it against a named reference. Row 1, permitted. ST-D1-045 reads *used responsibility language that mapped to the source's pre-defined support set*. The mapping is predicated of the participant, who never saw that set. Row 2, refused. **Polarity plays no part in either verdict.**

### 31.6 The qualification conditions

**¶31.31**  **Two routes exist and they have different conditions.** The artifact comparison exception at Section 31.4 governs entries whose subject is the artifact. The qualification route governs entries whose subject is the participant and which qualify the act against a named reference. **The four conditions of the exception do not apply to the qualification route**, because the first of them requires the artifact to be the subject, which this route by definition is not.

| Serial | Condition for a qualified entry |
|---|---|
| 1 | The act qualified is the participant's own, and the verb is theirs |
| 2 | The reference is named in the entry and was designated by the source before the observation |
| 3 | The comparison operator is declared, from Table 31.7, with its domain |
| 4 | The qualification asserts only the operator's result. It asserts no cause, no intent and no adequacy |
| 5 | The evidence establishing the operator's result is recorded with the determination |
| 6 | Where the participant's response is not independently available, the qualification states the operator result as determined rather than as verified, and the limitation is recorded |

**Table 31.10. The six conditions for a qualified entry.**

**¶31.32**  **Artifact preservation is not required on this route, and that is deliberate.** The exception at Section 31.4 authorizes an entry whose subject is the artifact, and for that the artifact must exist. A qualification predicates the act of the participant and qualifies it against a reference the source designated, so what must be preserved is the **reference and the determination's basis**, not a participant produced artifact. Condition 6 carries the consequence: without an independently available response, the entry says what was determined, not what a reader can check.

**¶31.33**  **A position considered and not taken.** It has been argued in review that any observer established comparison should be refused wherever it appears in a clause, including in a subordinate qualification. **This Standard does not adopt that rule.** Applied to D1 it would void ST-D1-031 through ST-D1-034, ST-D1-046, ST-D1-047 and ST-D1-071, which is most of what the dimension records about accounts, and it would leave the institution unable to record whether an account corresponded with the information available. The objection it rests on is real and is answered instead at Section 31.5: a comparison in a subordinate clause is permitted **only where its operator and domain are declared**, which makes it independently testable rather than a matter of grammar. A reviewer who finds the operator rule insufficient should say so against Section 31.5, not against the position of the clause.

## 32. Step 1 and Step 2. Read the bank, extract the recorded acts

**¶32.1**  **What you do.** Read every scenario for the dimension at every level. Not the titles. The narratives.

**¶32.2**  Strip from each one, before you consider it: the level and scoring labels, the competency list, the notes on what the scenario tests, the expected strong response, the red flag indicators, and any scoring guidance. **All of that tells you what quality the scenario was written to display. None of it tells you what act is recorded.**

**¶32.3**  **The decision at this step.** For each scenario, ask one question:

> If I had only a transcript of what the participant said and did, what could I state without forming a view?

**¶32.4**  Write down the act. Not the quality it displays.

**¶32.5**  **How you know the step is finished.** You have a list of acts, each of which could be established from a record by someone who was not present.

**¶32.6**  **What this produced in D1.** The dimension is named Integrity and Ethics, which suggests observing whether participants are honest. The scenarios do not contain honesty. They contain people identifying issues, raising them at particular moments, giving accounts, describing their own part, naming corrective actions, using routes, being questioned and answering again, and disclosing interests. Eight acts, none of which is a quality.

## 33. Step 3. Admit the conduct elements

**¶33.1**  **Steps 1 and 2 were discovery. This step is admission. They are separate activities and must not be collapsed.**

**¶33.2**  Discovery extracts candidate observable acts from the bank without imposing a desired quality. Admission decides which candidates become conduct elements, and it is decided against the construct, not against the bank alone. **A library consistent with its scenario bank can still fail to represent the dimension**, and this step is the only place that is caught.

**¶33.3**  **What you do.** For each candidate act, record its frequency in the bank, then apply the four admission criteria.

**¶33.4**  **Frequency is a discovery aid, not an admission rule.** The number of times an act appears in an authored bank reflects how many variants someone wrote, not how broadly the act matters to the dimension. Where several scenarios are rotations of one situation, frequency measures the rotation, not the conduct. A rare act may be central; a frequent one may be an artifact of authoring.

**¶33.5**  **The four admission criteria.**

**Table 33.1. The four admission criteria.**

| Serial | Criterion | Admit when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Construct relevance | The act falls within the dimension's approved construct rather than an adjacent dimension's |
| 2 | Evidentiary recoverability | The act can be established from a record by someone who was not present |
| 3 | Situational coverage | The act appears across materially distinct situations, not across rotations of one |
| 4 | Source admissibility | The sources carrying it can be served under the governing instruments |

**¶33.6**  **How frequency is used.** A high count in materially distinct situations is supporting evidence under 33.3. A high count across rotations of one situation is not. A low count does not exclude an act: where an act meets 33.1, 33.2 and 33.4 but appears in few scenarios, **admit it and record the thin coverage**, so the gap is visible rather than silently resolved by exclusion.

**¶33.7**  **How you know the step is finished.** Every candidate act has been admitted or excluded with its criterion recorded, and each thinly covered admission carries a note of its coverage.

**¶33.8**  **On the expected number.** Dimensions built to date have produced between seven and ten conduct elements. **That is a design expectation, not a requirement.** A dimension producing six or eleven is not thereby defective. An author who discards a qualifying act to reach the range has broken this step.

**¶33.9**  **A further decision: held back elements.** Some recurring acts must not be observed even though they qualify. An act is held back where observing it would record what was done to the participant rather than what the participant did, or would require recording a person's private life. Record each held back element and its reason. Do not build a question for it.

**¶33.10**  **What this produced in D1.** Eight conduct elements, mapping to question objects Q-D1-01 through Q-D1-08.

## 34. Step 4. Build the prohibited vocabulary

**¶34.1**  **This is the step that does the most work, and it cannot be done in advance.**

**¶34.2**  **What you do.** Begin drafting entries. As you draft, watch for the word that keeps slipping a conclusion back in. Write it down. The list accumulates as you draft; it is not predicted beforehand.

**¶34.3**  **The decision at this step.** For each draft entry, ask:

> Does this say what the participant did, or what someone concluded about them?

**¶34.4**  When the answer is "concluded," identify the specific word that did it. That word goes on the list.

**¶34.5**  **How you know the step is finished.** You can draft an entry without a word from the list appearing in it.

**¶34.6**  **Why each dimension's list is different.** The list is a record of what actually went wrong while drafting that dimension, not a generic caution to be objective.

**Table 34.1. Where the act test failed, by dimension.**

| Serial | Dimension | Where the act test kept failing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | D1 Integrity and Ethics | Nouns of character: honesty, integrity, courage |
| 2 | D2 Accountability and Ownership | Verbs of purpose: deflected, evaded, owned |
| 3 | D3 Execution Reliability | Comparatives: adequate, sufficient, thorough |
| 4 | D4 Communication Under Pressure | Adverbs of manner: calmly, clearly, defensively |
| 5 | D5 Collaboration and Conflict Resolution | Outcome verbs: resolved, settled, persuaded |
| 6 | D8 Resilience and Recovery | Words for how a person bore something: coped, recovered, unfazed |

**¶34.7**  The full list for each dimension is at its appendix.

**¶34.8**  **The rule that follows, and exactly what it scans.** An imprecise scan produces false refusals that erode trust in the control faster than a missing scan would.

**Table 34.2. The scan target.**

| Serial | Scanned | Not scanned |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The composed text of the entry, as approved | Section and dimension headings |
| 2 | The resolved value of a bound placeholder, where the source supplies free text | Field labels, identifiers and the report frame's own fixed wording |
| 3 | Any text composed into the Recorded Conduct field | The Recorded Contexts and Differences Across Settings fields, which are governed separately |

**¶34.9**  **Timing.** The entry's composed text is scanned **at authoring**, before approval, where a hit blocks approval. The resolved output is scanned **at render**, where a hit refuses and logs.

**¶34.10**  **Bound placeholders.** Where a placeholder resolves to a value drawn from a controlled list on the source sheet, the value is scanned once when the source is approved and not again at render. Where it resolves to free text, it is scanned at render.

**¶34.11**  **False refusals.** Every refusal is logged with the word that triggered it. A word producing repeated refusals on legitimate output is reviewed against the list rather than worked around, and removed from the list by amendment under Part E if it does not belong there.

## 35. Step 5. Write the question objects

**¶35.1**  **What you do.** One question per conduct element. Write the answer set before writing any entry.

**¶35.2**  **The four rules for an answer set.**

**Table 35.1. The four rules for an answer set.**

| Serial | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mutually exclusive | Two answers that could both be true make the determination undecidable |
| 2 | Exhaustive | An observer facing an unanticipated case has no correct answer |
| 3 | Terminated by a not observed answer | Without it, an observer facing an absence must choose a wrong answer |
| 4 | No answer a superset of another | A superset answer silently absorbs its subset |

**¶35.3**  **The not observed terminal records one cause only, and the others are governed elsewhere.** A single terminal answer must not absorb materially different reasons an entry cannot be admitted. The terminal answer in an answer set means exactly one thing: **the act was not observed despite an established opportunity.**

**Table 35.2. The seven causes and where each is recorded.**

| Serial | Cause | Where it is recorded |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The act was not observed despite an established opportunity | The answer set's not observed terminal, as a record status entry |
| 2 | No valid opportunity existed | The separately governed opportunity state. Never the terminal answer |
| 3 | The administration was interrupted or invalid | The separately governed administration state |
| 4 | An accessibility barrier prevented response | The separately governed accessibility state |
| 5 | The participant did not respond at all | The separately governed nonresponse state |
| 6 | The evidence is unresolved or insufficient | The separately governed insufficiency state |
| 7 | The source does not carry this element | Source applicability. The question is not served |

**¶35.4**  **Why this matters.** Treating 35.10 as though it were 35.9 records a participant as not having acted when they were never given the chance. That is the failure the evidence chain at row 8.1 exists to prevent, and an answer set that absorbs all seven causes into one terminal defeats it.

**¶35.5**  **The decision at this step.** For each answer, ask whether a determination landing there is distinguishable from every other answer by evidence alone. Where two answers would be distinguished only by an observer's judgment of degree, collapse them.

**¶35.6**  **How you know the step is finished.** Every question has between three and five answers, exactly one of which is the not observed terminal.

**¶35.7**  **What this produced in D1.** Q-D1-02, timing of raising the issue, carries four answers.

**Table 35.3. The four answers of Q-D1-02.**

| Serial | Answer | Distinguished by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Before the enquiry | No prompt precedes the raising |
| 2 | After the enquiry, in response to it | A prompt precedes it and it answers the prompt |
| 3 | After the enquiry, not in response to it | A prompt precedes it and it does not answer the prompt |
| 4 | Not within the observed period | Terminal |

**¶35.8**  Each is distinguished by the record alone: whether a prompt preceded, and whether the raising answered it. None requires a judgment of degree.

## 36. Step 6. Write the entries

**¶36.1**  **What you do.** One entry per answer. Apply Section 18, **the four tests at Section 31** — scope, subject, assertion decomposition and factual status — and the prohibited vocabulary from Step 4.

**¶36.2**  **The drafting sequence for a single entry.**

**Table 36.1. The drafting sequence for one entry.**

| Serial | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Write the act in plain words, **stored beginning in lower case** so the entry composes anywhere. The report face capitalizes its first character at render |
| 2 | Apply the subject test at Section 31.2. Is the subject the participant and the verb their act, or does a defined exception apply? |
| 2a | **Apply the assertion decomposition test at Section 31.3.** List every fact the wording asserts or presupposes and name the evidence for each. Any row without evidence refuses the entry |
| 2b | **Apply the factual status check at Table 31.5.** Confirm the entry preserves the modality, actuality, attribution, completion and directness of the underlying response |
| 3 | Apply the scope test. Would this be accurate in another industry, and does it still say what was done? |
| 4 | Scan against the prohibited vocabulary |
| 5 | Check it asserts nothing from Table 16.1 |
| 6 | Check it asserts one proposition, not two |
| 7 | Check it is distinguishable from its sibling entries by evidence alone |

**¶36.3**  **Permitted verbs.** Verbs of saying and doing, attributable to the participant: stated, identified, raised, described, referred to, used, attributed, disclosed, added. Not verbs of quality, outcome, manner or conclusion.

**¶36.4**  **The not observed entry.** Every terminal answer gets an entry, and these are written differently. They do not describe the participant at all; they describe the record.

**Table 36.2. Four D1 not observed entries.**

| Serial | Entry | Composed text |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ST-D1-013 | no reference to an issue was observed within the recorded period |
| 2 | ST-D1-024 | no mention of the issue was observed within the recorded period |
| 3 | ST-D1-035 | no account was observed within the recorded period |
| 4 | ST-D1-043 | no statement about the participant's own involvement was observed within the recorded period |

**¶36.5**  Note the construction: *no X was observed within the recorded period*, never *the participant did not X*. The first asserts an absence in the record. The second asserts an absence in the participant. **Only the first is supportable by the evidence.** The second claims the participant did not act, which the record cannot establish, because the record can only show that no act was observed.

**¶36.6**  **Naming the limit.** Every entry that depends on a boundary must name it. D1 does this with "within the recorded period," "defined for the source," and "the source provided information identified for this observation." An entry that depends on an unstated boundary cannot be challenged, because a participant cannot see what it was measured against.

**¶36.7**  **How you know the step is finished.** Every answer in every question has exactly one entry, and every entry has passed Table 36.1.

## 37. Step 7. Add bound placeholders

**¶37.1**  **What you do.** Identify the entries where a specific genuinely must appear, and bind it rather than asserting it.

**¶37.2**  **The decision at this step.**

**Table 37.1. The bound placeholder decision.**

| Serial | Situation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The specific is not needed for the reader to understand the act | Leave it out. ST-D1-011 |
| 2 | The specific is needed, and the source can supply it | Bind it to a placeholder |
| 3 | The specific is needed and the source cannot supply it | The entry cannot be authored |

**¶37.3**  **What this produced in D1.** Two entries carry placeholders.

> ST-D1-032: the account was consistent with the source provided information identified for this observation, and did not include `{omitted_items}`.

> ST-D1-061: the participant raised the issue through `{route_used}`.

**¶37.4**  In both, the entry stays portable and the instance carries the particular.

**¶37.5**  **Three rules for placeholders.**

**Table 37.2. Placeholder rules.**

### 37.1 The placeholder content contract

**¶37.6**  **A placeholder carries a label, never a proposition.** Nothing in the rules above constrains what a value may contain, and an unconstrained value can carry an assertion the entry was never approved to make.

**¶37.7**  **Worked failure.** The approved template reads *the participant raised the issue through {route_used}*. A source supplies the value *the reporting channel, which prevented further circulation of the figures*. The resolved entry asserts a consequence, which Section 16 prohibits, and every check in Part D passes: the template holds no prohibited word, the field exists, the value is non empty, and determinacy resolves.

> A placeholder accepts an approved label only. It must not introduce another person's conduct, a consequence, a causal claim, an evaluative modifier, a temporal or conditional qualifier, or any further independent proposition. Unrestricted free text may not occupy a placeholder.

**¶37.8**  Each placeholder carries a declared value grammar or an approved value set. A value outside it refuses and logs.

### 37.2 Substitution closure

**¶37.9**  **Approval attaches to the resolved proposition, not to the template.** An entry is not approved until every permitted substitution has been tested in its composed form.

| Serial | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Every value in the approved set is composed into the entry and the resolved clause tested against Sections 31.1, 31.2, 31.3 and 16 |
| 2 | Where the value set is open, the value grammar is tested at its permitted limits |
| 3 | The resolved clause is scanned against the prohibited vocabulary at render, in addition to the value being scanned at source approval |
| 4 | Source field approval does not substitute for either test |

**Table 37.3. The four substitution closure requirements.**

**¶37.10**  **Why source approval is not enough.** A controlled value may be admissible as source data and still produce an inadmissible entry when composed. The two judgments are made against different things by different instruments, and neither stands in for the other.

| Serial | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | A placeholder is named for what it holds, in lower case with underscores |
| 2 | Its source field must exist on the source sheet. Integrity is checked on load |
| 3 | Where the source field is absent or empty, the entry refuses and logs. It never renders an empty slot |

## 38. What an entry need not carry

**¶38.1**  Before verifying, apply this. It prevents the most common authoring error, which is cramming every material fact into the common wording.

**¶38.2**  **The adjacent field principle.** The report has a Differences Across Settings field. Material variation that the common entry would otherwise conceal is preserved there, not inside the entry.

**¶38.3**  **Worked example from D1.** A participant raises an issue before enquiry at Stage 1 and in response to enquiry at Stage 2. Both support the common proposition that the issue was raised. The timing differs materially.

**Table 38.1. Recurrence and difference held in separate fields.**

| Serial | Field | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recorded Conduct | The common entry, where the proposition is supported in both contexts |
| 2 | Differences Across Settings | Stage 1, raised before enquiry. Stage 2, raised in response to enquiry. Neither preferred, averaged, nor used to broaden the common entry |

**¶38.4**  **This is what lets an entry be general without being thin.** The entry does not have to carry every material fact, because a companion field preserves what the common wording would hide.

**¶38.5**  **But acceptance is not distributed, even though ownership is.**

> Construction boundedness is tested on the complete rendered claim package: the entry, its frame, its required context, its material qualifiers and its stated limitations. **A required qualifier that is missing, inaccurate, non adjacent or insufficient is a construction defect**, even where another instrument owns the field it should have appeared in.

**¶38.6**  **Worked failure.** The same entry is rendered for two participants. In one the issue was volunteered; in the other the source named it and the participant repeated it. The common act may properly be reported. If the prompt condition is not actually carried in the adjacent field, the package asserts a common act while omitting a condition its own evidence rules treat as material. **That is the construction failing, not a reader inventing an inference.**

**¶38.7**  Each entry therefore declares the context fields its proposition depends on, and the render refuses where a declared dependency is absent.

**¶38.8**  **The evidence state label is part of the claim and is tested with it.** An entry supported in three contexts, rendered under a recurrence label, can communicate repeated independent conduct where the contexts differed materially in prompting or mode. The common act may be properly supported and the package still overclaim. **The adjacent difference field exists to prevent this; its existence does not establish that it did.** Test the label, the frame, the entry, the recorded contexts, the differences and the limitations together, against synthetic cases built for the purpose.

## 40. Step 9. Build the recurrence keys

**¶40.1**  **What you do.** One key per conduct element. Each key states the bounded proposition claimed to be the same across two contexts.

**Table 40.1. The five fields of a recurrence key.**

| Serial | Field | Holds |

# PART E — AMENDING AN EXISTING LIBRARY

**¶40.6**  Part E governs change to an entry that already exists. Like Part D, it is a procedure.

**¶40.7**  **Why this part carries more weight than it appears to.** The authoring procedure runs once per dimension. The amendment procedure runs every time the pilot returns a finding, every time a defect is noticed, and every time a recipient misreads an entry. Most of the lifetime use of this Standard is Part E.

## 41. What counts as a change

**¶41.1**  Three kinds, and they are governed differently.

**Table 41.1. The five kinds of change.**

### 41.1 The maintenance route

**¶41.2**  **Some compliant changes fit none of the first three classes.** An entry correctly stating that calibration evidence does not yet exist, to which an approved study is later attached, was not defective, has not changed its proposition, and is not withdrawn. A required property has nonetheless changed and eligibility may have moved.

> Non semantic property and evidence updates use the controlled maintenance route. The record identifies the changed fields, the approval, the version and the effect on state under Section 45. The route cannot change composed meaning, question and answer mapping, admission or withholding conditions, exclusions, placeholders or recurrence rules. A change touching any of those is a correction, a revision or a withdrawal, whatever it is called.

**¶41.3**  **Attaching evidence is not the same as clearing a gate.** The maintenance route records that evidence now exists. Whether that evidence is sufficient for a change of state is a separate decision, made under Section 45 and recorded as such.

| Serial | Kind | Definition | Governance |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Correction | The entry does not comply with this Standard and never did. Its meaning is unchanged by the fix | New version. Impact review limited to rendering |
| 2 | Revision | The entry's proposition changes. What it asserts is now different | New version. Full impact review of affected records |
| 3 | Withdrawal | The entry must no longer be used | New version. Full impact review. Identifier retired, never reused |
| 4 | Maintenance | A required property changes without altering the proposition: evidence attached, a reference updated, a field populated | Controlled maintenance route at Section 41.1. No semantic reclassification |
| 5 | Addition | A new entry enters a library already in use | New version. Section 43 tests, then the activation route at Section 43.1. Full impact review of the affected answer set |

**¶41.4**  **The test that separates a correction from a revision.** Ask:

> Every admissible case supporting the old proposition must support the new, **and** every admissible case supporting the new must support the old, under the same evidence conditions.

**¶41.5**  Both directions must hold. Where both hold it is a correction. Where either fails it is a revision, and every record carrying the entry must be reviewed.

**¶41.6**  **Why one direction is not enough.** A one directional test admits a broadening. Take *the participant raised the issue before the direct question* amended to *the participant raised the issue*. Every case supporting the old wording supports the new, so a one way test returns correction with impact review limited to rendering. But a prompted disclosure supports the new wording and fails the old. **The supported case set has grown, which is a revision and a breach of Section 44.** The prompted disclosure is the witness that proves it.

**¶41.7**  **What the test must preserve.** Equivalence is not only of wording. A correction preserves the exclusions, the withheld cases, the material qualifiers, the question and answer mapping and the recurrence eligibility. A change to any of those is a revision whatever the wording does.

**¶41.8**  **Record a counterexample search in both directions.** Where none is found, record that none was found. An unexamined direction is not a passed direction.

**¶41.9**  **Worked application to the two D1 capitalization defects.** ST-D1-083 and ST-D1-083b begin with a capital where their siblings do not. A determination correctly made against the old wording is still correctly expressed by the lower case version. **Correction.** Impact review limited to rendering.

**¶41.10**  **Worked application to ST-D1-045.** The entry states a mapping rather than an act. Correcting the subject changes what the entry asserts: from *the observer established a correspondence* to *the participant attributed responsibility in terms the source provides*. A determination made against the old wording may not be correctly expressed by the new. **Revision.** Full impact review.

## 42. The amendment procedure

**Table 42.1. The ten amendment steps.**

**¶42.1**  **Why the classification is performed twice.** A trigger is not a classification. An author who classifies at Step 2 and then, while drafting, removes a prompt condition or adds a material qualifier has produced a revision carrying a correction label, and every later step inherits the lighter treatment. The binding classification is made against the finished wording. **Any change after it invalidates it and the step is repeated.**

| Serial | Step | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Record the trigger | What caused the change: a pilot finding, a defect, a participant challenge, a recipient misreading. Record it before drafting |
| 2 | Classify the change provisionally | Correction, revision, withdrawal or maintenance, using the tests at Section 41. **This classification is provisional and binds nothing** |
| 3 | Draft the new wording | Apply Table 36.1 in full. An amended entry is authored to the same standard as a new one |
| 3a | Re classify against the frozen package | Classify the exact old and new pair, with every dependency, once the wording is final. A reviewer who did not draft it performs this classification. **It is this classification that binds, and it binds both version digests** |
| 4 | Re run determinacy | Table 39.1 across the whole library, not only the changed entry |
| 5 | Check the recurrence keys | Does the entry still support the key's bounded proposition? Section 45 of the dimension's library |
| 6 | Run the four expansion tests | Where the change adds a distinction rather than fixing one. Section 43 |
| 7 | Run the impact assessment | Identify every record carrying the entry and every downstream artifact affected, at the depth the classification requires. **This precedes issue** |
| 8 | Issue the new version | With the reason recorded, the prior version retained, and the impact assessment attached |
| 9 | Complete post release verification | Confirm the issued version renders as assessed and that affected records were handled as the assessment required |

**¶42.2**  **The sequence is deliberate.** Assessing impact after issuing a version means the library is already in use before anyone knows what the change touched. Where the assessment reveals that a revision would affect records that cannot be reprocessed, the correct outcome may be to withdraw rather than revise, and that decision must be available before issue, not after.

**¶42.3**  **What must remain traceable after any change.**

**Table 42.2. Traceability requirements.**

### 42.1 Approved dependencies

**¶42.4**  **An entry's approval covers more than its wording.** A change to a dependency alters the proposition the institution publishes while leaving the template untouched, and nothing in the steps above would notice.

> An entry's approved dependencies are its placeholder schema, its permitted value set or value grammar, the source field versions it draws on, its question and answer mapping, its composition rules, **the admission and withholding conditions that govern it, the comparison operator and domain where it has one, and the defined meaning of every term its question relies on**. A change to any dependency triggers an impact classification for that entry, even where the composed template is unchanged.

**¶42.5**  **Worked failure.** A question distinguishes disclosure before a direct question from disclosure afterward. An author redefines *direct question* to exclude indirect prompting. The entry still reads *the participant raised the issue before being asked* and not one character has changed. **A response previously classified as prompted is now classified as unprompted.** What the institution determines has changed; what it publishes has not. Without this rule nothing in Part E would notice.

**¶42.7**  This does not duplicate source governance. It defines the interface at which a change on the source side alters what the report says.

**¶43.1**  An addition is a new entry in a library already in use. It must pass the four tests from Section 11 before it is authored.

**Table 43.1. The four tests for an addition.**

### 43.1 The activation route

**¶43.2**  **Passing the four tests authorizes the entry. It does not activate it.** An entry added to an answer set already in use changes that set, and the consequences fall on entries nobody edited.

| Serial | Consequence to assess before activation | Refuse activation where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Exhaustiveness of the answer set | The set was exhaustive and the addition overlaps an existing answer |
| 2 | Mutual exclusivity | A determination could now land on two answers |
| 3 | Existing question and answer mappings | Any existing entry's mapping changes |
| 4 | Recurrence key eligibility | The key's bounded proposition no longer holds for every eligible entry |

**Table 43.2. The six activation consequences.**

### 43.2 Historical non admission

**¶43.3**  **Consequence 5 is the one authors miss, and it is the one that must not refuse an activation.** Where an answer set gains an option, every prior case recorded as not observed must be examined to establish whether it would now resolve to the new entry.

> Where it would, the prior records are **not** retrospectively amended and the activation is **not** refused. The activation record states that historical non admission carries a different meaning before and after this version, the entry's version history says so, and every affected prior record is listed.

**¶43.4**  **Why this is versioned rather than refused.** Refusing on this ground would make any library in use permanently unextendable, because almost every addition changes what a past withholding would have meant. **The institution's answer to a changed meaning is a version boundary, not a prohibition.** An earlier form of this Standard listed it as a refusal ground and described the versioning response in the same section, giving opposite dispositions for one case.

**¶43.5**  **Consequences 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 do refuse.** They describe a set that would become internally inconsistent, or an issued report whose meaning would change. Neither can be resolved by a version boundary, because one breaks the determinacy the library depends on and the other alters a document already in someone's hands.

**¶43.6**  **An addition is a revision of the answer set, not only an addition to the library.** It carries revision level governance under Part E whatever the four tests at Table 43.1 returned.

| Serial | Test | The question asked | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evidentiary necessity | Does this capture a distinction the governed source evidence actually establishes? | The distinction exists only in the author's mind |
| 2 | Calibration feasibility | Can independent observers apply this consistently? | Two observers would reasonably differ on the same record |
| 3 | Participant fairness | Does this preserve transparency, challenge rights, and protection against unsupported characterization? | A participant could not tell what it was measured against |
| 4 | Demonstrated usefulness | Does this respond to an identified need of the report's intended users? | It improves the report's appearance without improving its evidentiary usefulness |

**¶43.7**  An addition that increases descriptive richness while reducing factual traceability weakens the model and must not be made.

## 44. The prohibition on retrospective broadening

**¶44.1**  **The rule.** A recurrence proposition is defined before the relevant observations are compared. It is never broadened afterward.

**¶44.2**  **The failure this prevents.** An author compares records, finds they do not support the common proposition, and widens the wording until they do. The result appears consistent and asserts something the evidence never established.

**¶44.3**  **The procedure.** Where records do not support an existing proposition, the available actions are to record them as not recurrent, or to define a **narrower** proposition and test it from the start against all records. Widening an existing proposition to accommodate records already seen is prohibited.

**¶44.4**  **Narrowing is defined by admissible case set, never by sentence length.**

> A proposition is narrowed only where its admissible case set is a subset of the original. Removing a material condition broadens the set even where the sentence becomes shorter and says less.

**¶44.5**  **Worked failure.** Records hold disclosures at differing times and do not support *raised the issue before being asked*. An author removes the timing condition and offers *raised the issue* as a narrower proposition because it claims less. It claims less and **admits more**, so it is a broadening, and calling it narrowing uses this very paragraph to defeat the rule above it.

**¶44.6**  **Prior inspection cannot be undone by re testing.** A proposition formed after looking at the records is exploratory and must be recorded as such. Testing it from the start against those same records does not make it prospectively specified. Any retrospective descriptive use requires its own approved rule and is never reported as a recurrence result.

### 45.1 What eligibility attaches to

**¶45.4**  **Eligibility binds a package, not an identifier.** A revised entry that keeps its identifier would otherwise keep a calibration reference obtained for the wording it no longer has.

> Eligibility binds the exact entry version together with its question and answer mapping, admission and withholding conditions, material variable rules, placeholders and value sets, recurrence rules, and the approved evidence package. A material change suspends consequential use of the changed package until the gates for the new package are cleared.

**¶45.5**  **Evidence does not carry forward by reuse of an identifier.** It carries forward only through a recorded equivalence decision, approved by someone who did not make the change, stating why the earlier evidence bears on the new package.

### 45.2 Pilot serving

**¶45.6**  **A real participant pilot is not production, and it is not synthetic.** The three states would otherwise leave no lawful route to the evidence production eligibility requires: calibration ready permits calibration exercises, production eligible permits real serving, and the evidence for the second is produced by activity that looks like the first.

> Calibration ready entries may be served to real participants **only under an identified approved pilot authorization**, carrying its own consent, evidence handling, participant review and output restrictions. **That permission does not confer production eligibility and does not become it by repetition.**

| Serial | Pilot serving permits | Pilot serving does not permit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Serving a real participant under the pilot authorization | Serving outside the authorized pilot population |
| 2 | Producing an observation record marked as pilot output | Producing a report for consequential use |
| 3 | Releasing output to a participant under the pilot's review terms | Releasing to an employer except where the pilot authorization expressly provides for it and the participant consents |
| 4 | Generating the calibration and accuracy evidence Section 45 requires | Treating accumulated pilot volume as evidence of eligibility |

**Table 45.3. What pilot serving permits and forbids.**

**¶45.7**  **The output carries its state.** Anything produced from a calibration ready entry is marked as pilot output on its face and in its record, and the mark is not removable by a later change of state. A record produced before an entry was production eligible does not become a production record when it becomes one.

**¶45.8**  **Calibration evidence is required for production eligibility only.** It is never required to enter calibration. Requiring it would be circular.

**¶45.9**  Where an entry has not met the requirements for a state, any activity reserved to that state refuses and logs. **Those refusals are the designed first state, not defects.** They are what prevents an unverified library from being used for a consequential purpose, while allowing the work that produces the verification to proceed.

**¶45.10**  **A failed check is not cleared by the status it would otherwise allow.** Section 39, check 5 of Table 39.1, records that ST-D1-083 and ST-D1-083b fail the uniform composition check. An earlier form of this table declared Authoring complete met for D1 regardless, which described a failed acceptance criterion as passed. **The requirement is marked not met and the defects are listed at Section 47.**

**¶45.11**  **Building continues.** Row 1 of Table 45.1 permits building, loading, synthetic rendering and demonstration at every state, including before Authoring complete is reached. Nothing in the estate stops because a check failed; what stops is the claim that it passed.

Introduce no new stack. Where no repository is present, build nothing and record `no_codebase_present`.

**¶49.10**  **What this Part does not govern.** It does not govern what a Recorded Conduct Entry says. | 2 | Validate the value against the entry's `placeholder_grammar`: permitted set membership and type | `bound_value_invalid` where it is outside the set or of the wrong type |

| 4 | Substitute once. **A substituted value is never itself scanned for further placeholders** | `placeholder_unresolved` where any brace pair remains **anywhere in the resolved output**, from the template or from a value |
| 5 | Scan the **resolved** clause against the dimension's prohibited vocabulary, matching whole words and whole phrases, case insensitively, on word boundaries | `prohibited_term_in_resolved_entry` |

**Table 56.1. The five steps of placeholder resolution.**

**¶56.2**  **A placeholder carries a label, never a proposition.** A grammar permits an approved label only. It must not admit another person's conduct, a consequence, a causal claim, an evaluative modifier, a temporal or conditional qualifier, or any further independent proposition. **Unrestricted free text may not occupy a placeholder**, and a grammar that would admit it is itself a defect.

**¶56.3**  **Source approval is not substitution approval.** A value may be admissible as source data and still produce an inadmissible entry when composed. Step 5 is performed at render, on the resolved clause, in addition to any scan performed when the source was approved.

### 57.9 The single draw path

**¶57.38**  **This rule exists because a build following every other rule in this Part still produced a page with text 37 points past the right margin.**

> All text is drawn through **one function** that sets the character spacing explicitly on every call, including to zero. The renderer never sets character spacing and leaves it set.

**¶57.39**  **Why.** Character spacing is part of the PDF text state, and the text state persists across text blocks. A letterspaced heading sets it once and **every line drawn afterwards is wider than the line breaker measured**, because the breaker measures with zero spacing. The page looks subtly wrong, no other rule in this Part is violated, and nothing refuses.

| Serial | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | One draw function. No direct text calls anywhere else in the renderer |
| 2 | Character spacing set on every call, explicitly, including to zero |
| 3 | The line breaker and the draw path use **the same font, size and spacing values**, from the same source |
| 4 | Any other persistent text state the output format carries, such as word spacing or horizontal scaling, is set explicitly on the same call |

**Table 57.11. The single draw path.**

**¶57.40**  **The symptom to look for.** Lines that fit when measured and overrun when drawn, with the overrun growing down the page. **Verify by measuring the rendered output, not by looking at it.**

## 59. Refusals and acceptance

### 59.1 Three classes of failure

**¶59.1**  **A result belongs to exactly one class, and the class decides what survives.** Three classes are failures. **A fourth class, Advisory, is not a failure**: it records a measurement or an expected condition, stops nothing, and is never returned as a refusal.

| Serial | Class | Raised when | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Package | A resource in the execution manifest is absent, ambiguous or unresolvable | The service does not start. No render is attempted |
| 2 | Render | A rule governing the face is violated | **No report face artifact is produced.** No partial face, ever |
| 3 | Persistence | A record may not be created or altered | **An already valid artifact is not destroyed.** Only the record operation is refused |

**Table 59.1. The three failure classes.**

**¶59.2**  **This resolves a contradiction in an earlier form of this Part**, which said every refusal produces no face while also requiring a demonstration face to survive a refused persistence. **¶59.3**  **A validation pass reports every failure it finds, not the first.** Where a single primary code must be returned, precedence is the order of Table 59.2, lowest serial first.

### 59.4 Comparison against the reference page

**¶59.6**  **A build is conformant when it reproduces the reference page from `FIX-SPECIMEN`, not when it satisfies the rules separately.** The rules can all pass and the page still differ.

| Serial | Comparison | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Structure | One US Letter page, 612.0 by 792.0 |
| 2 | Text | Every word identical in content and punctuation. **Apostrophes are U+0027, the straight form, everywhere.** Typographic quotes are prohibited in controlled text, and a document pipeline that converts them silently is a defect in the pipeline |
| 3 | Position | Every word's box within **0.5 points** of the reference in x and y |
| 4 | Fonts | **Every font that draws a glyph** is Liberation Sans Regular, Bold or Italic, embedded, at the sizes and weights of Table 57.5. A font resource that is declared and never draws a glyph is a library artifact and is not compared: two correct builds differ there |

**¶59.9**  **Tests 99 to 105 are what separate a renderer from a copier.** A build that reproduces the reference page and accepts every one of those seven inputs has proved it can draw, and nothing else. **Run them before trusting any output.**

## Research and governance qualification

**¶61.40**  This Standard sets out the institutional rationale for bounded recorded conduct and the governing procedure for authoring and amending Recorded Conduct Entries.

**¶61.41**  The cited literature provides methodological guidance concerning interpretation, transfer, observer agreement, factual accuracy, fairness and the evaluation of evidence based claims.

**¶61.42**  **None of the cited publications independently validates The 3rd Academy's observation pathway, observer calibration procedures, Recorded Conduct Libraries, or Behavioral Evidence Report.**

**¶61.43**  Specific claims about the performance of those systems remain subject to controlled implementation testing, independent review and empirical evidence. In particular, this Standard **specifies the requirements for construction boundedness**. It does not, by its own existence, establish that an entry meets them.

**¶61.44**  Compliance requires a demonstrated entailment from the governed determination and its required context to the **complete resolved entry**, together with the assertion decomposition at Section 31.3, the prohibited assertion checks at Section 16, and the substitution closure at Section 37.2. **Passing a vocabulary scan or a grammatical test does not establish compliance**, and an earlier version of this Standard claimed more than its own procedures could deliver.

**¶61.45**  Whether entries are bounded in reception is a separate and empirical question. The controlled pilot can identify particular misinterpretations and estimate how often they occur under the conditions studied. **It cannot establish that no reasonable recipient could ever overread an entry**, and no finding from it may be reported as though it had.

**¶61.46**  This Standard does not independently authorize a new observation rule, evidence state, reporting feature, research use or commercial claim. Where its provisions intersect an approved governing instrument, that instrument retains its established authority.
